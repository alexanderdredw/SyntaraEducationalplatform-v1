Bread, meat and an apple cannot go straight into the blood: their molecules are too big. The digestive system turns food into simple substances that the body can absorb.

## Why We Digest Food {#functions}

Food contains proteins, fats and carbohydrates. These are large molecules, and they cannot pass through the wall of the intestine. During digestion they are broken down into simple substances:

- proteins into amino acids;
- fats into glycerol and fatty acids;
- complex carbohydrates such as starch into glucose.

Simple substances are absorbed into the blood and carried around the whole body. **Enzymes** — special proteins in the digestive juices — help break food down.

## Mouth and Oesophagus {#mouth}

Digestion begins in the mouth. Teeth grind the food, the tongue mixes it and saliva moistens it. Enzymes in saliva start breaking down starch — that is why bread tastes slightly sweet if you chew it for a long time.

The swallowed lump of food enters the **oesophagus**, a muscular tube about 25 centimetres long. Its walls contract in waves and push the food into the stomach.

## Stomach {#stomach}

The stomach is a muscular bag where food stays for several hours. Its walls produce **gastric juice**:

- **hydrochloric acid** kills many of the bacteria that come with food;
- **enzymes** start breaking down proteins.

The stomach muscles mix the food with the juice, and it turns into a liquid paste. The paste passes into the intestine in small portions.

## Intestines {#intestines}

**The small intestine** begins with the duodenum. Bile from the liver and juice from the pancreas enter here, and food is fully broken down. The inner surface of the small intestine is covered with millions of **villi**: through them nutrients are absorbed into the blood and lymph.

**The large intestine** absorbs water. It is home to helpful bacteria that help digest plant fibre and make some vitamins. Undigested remains of food leave the body.

## Liver and Pancreas {#glands}

**The liver** is the largest gland in the body. It produces bile, which breaks fats into tiny droplets, neutralises harmful substances and stores glucose as glycogen.

**The pancreas** releases digestive juice with enzymes into the intestine and the hormone insulin into the blood, which controls the level of glucose.

To keep your digestion healthy, eat at regular times, do not rush, chew your food well and wash your hands before meals.
