Every second the heart pushes blood into the vessels, and the vessels carry it to every cell of the body. Together they form the cardiovascular system — the body's transport network.

## Main Functions {#functions}

The cardiovascular system keeps blood moving through the body. Thanks to this:

- cells receive oxygen and nutrients;
- carbon dioxide and other waste products are carried away from the tissues;
- hormones are delivered to the organs they control;
- heat is spread around the body, so its temperature stays constant;
- immune cells quickly reach the places that need protection.

## Components of the Cardiovascular System {#components}

The system has two parts.

**The heart** is a hollow muscular organ about the size of a fist. It works as a pump and pushes blood into the vessels.

**Blood vessels** are a network of tubes that blood flows through:

- **arteries** carry blood away from the heart;
- **veins** bring blood back to the heart;
- **capillaries** are the thinnest vessels; they connect arteries and veins.

About 5 litres of blood circulate in an adult's vessels.

## Structure of the Heart {#heart}

![Cross-section diagram of the heart: two atria at the top, two ventricles at the bottom, valves between them; the aorta leaves the left ventricle](media/heart.png)

The heart lies in the chest, slightly to the left of the middle. Inside it is divided into four chambers:

- two **atria** — right and left — receive blood from the veins;
- two **ventricles** — right and left — pump blood into the arteries.

A solid wall, the septum, keeps the blood of the right and left halves from mixing. **Valves** sit between the atria and the ventricles and at the exits of the ventricles. They open in one direction only, so blood cannot flow backwards.

The wall of the left ventricle is the thickest: it pumps blood through the whole body.

## Circulatory Pathways {#circulation}

Blood moves along two closed routes — the circulations.

**Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lung capillaries → pulmonary veins → left atrium. In the lungs blood gives off carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen.

**Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → aorta → arteries → organ capillaries → veins → venae cavae → right atrium. In the organs blood gives up oxygen and nutrients and collects waste products.

In one full circuit blood passes through the heart twice.

## Blood Vessels and Pressure Regulation {#vessels-pressure}

![Diagram of three kinds of vessels: an artery with a thick wall, a vein with a valve and a thin capillary where red blood cells pass one at a time](media/vessels.png)

Arteries have thick, elastic walls: they withstand the high pressure of the blood pushed out by the heart. Veins have thinner walls, and many veins in the arms and legs have valves that help blood rise back to the heart. A capillary wall is a single layer of cells, so substances pass through it from the blood into the tissues and back.

**Blood pressure** is the force of blood pushing on the vessel walls. It is measured with a blood pressure monitor and written as two numbers, for example 120/80 mm Hg: the first is the pressure when the heart contracts, the second when it relaxes. Pressure rises during exercise and excitement and falls at rest.

**The pulse** is the throb of an artery wall with each heartbeat. In a resting adult it is about 60–80 beats per minute.

## Microcirculation {#microcirculation}

Microcirculation is the flow of blood through the smallest vessels: arterioles, capillaries and venules. Capillaries are so narrow that red blood cells pass through them one at a time. Here oxygen and nutrients leave the blood for the tissues, and carbon dioxide and waste enter it. Tiny rings of muscle at the start of the capillaries open and close to send more blood to the organs that are working hardest.
